# Commercial thesis

## Repository-supported conclusion

**Promising but unproven.** Strongest concise thesis: a local evidence workspace for following a narrow subject over time, preserving source versions and exact supporting passages, tracking corrections and material Story changes, and answering questions against accumulated evidence.

Best initial audience: an individual analyst, investigative researcher or serious subject specialist who repeatedly revisits the same information need and must explain why a conclusion changed. The painful job is maintaining a defensible longitudinal record across changing, duplicated and contradictory sources. It is not simply reading today's headlines.

Differentiated implemented mechanisms include exact artifact/span provenance, source dependency context, correction/membership histories, temporal reads, evidence-bound report revisions, and refusal when evidence is insufficient. These could outperform a flat feed or one-off answer for longitudinal questions. No competitor comparison or superiority claim is established by repository code alone.

RSS/alerts provide a cheaper baseline for “tell me something new”; generic search/AI interfaces provide a plausible baseline for “answer this now.” Newsroom earns its extra setup only if accumulated structure improves decisions or saves repeated verification work. Its own Full-vs-Lite protocol is the correct internal test of that incremental value; it is not external market validation.

Recurring loop: set a narrow Watch → review meaningful changes → inspect evidence → record/ask a follow-up → return when new evidence changes a Story/report. Plausible paid value is reliable monitoring plus retained, inspectable analysis and time saved. Switching friction could come from a user's curated evidence/notes/correction history and workflow, not locking data or credentials into an opaque system. Keep export usable.

Weak commercial features unless proved: generic agent autonomy, intricate hypothesis tooling, raw job/budget dashboards, many taxonomy types, phase-derived diagnostics, and adding provider logos. Technical sophistication without useful output has little standalone value.

## External market hypotheses requiring research

These are hypotheses, not sourced market facts: specialists may pay for reliable local control and longitudinal evidence; they may instead prefer existing research/monitoring products with less setup; Windows-first may constrain distribution; source licensing/support may dominate economics. No external competitor collision study, market sizing, pricing study or user interviews were performed. Technical credential documentation was the only external research in this audit.

## Strongest argument against the product

The user may spend more time configuring, correcting and operating Newsroom than the system saves. Exact quotations can preserve page navigation or weak metadata just as faithfully as useful evidence. If the source-to-useful-Story yield remains low, the rigorous architecture becomes an expensive way to organize noise. A simpler feed plus search may deliver comparable outcomes with much lower support cost.

## Fastest validation

Preserve the approved UAP trial and the separate frozen historical comparison. Measure useful items per reviewed item, time to first value, repeated return, correction burden, missed material changes, evidence quality and active-observation time. Obtain actual human usefulness judgments; never score on the owner's behalf. Apply the unchanged three-of-five-category decision rule with trust guardrails. If inconclusive, extend or narrow the test rather than claim success. After demonstrated owner value, test a small specialist pilot and explicit purchase intent; let results decide price and feature scope.

Do not build multi-tenancy, cloud hosting, team permissions, billing infrastructure, generic autonomous agents or a wide provider catalog before this evidence exists.
